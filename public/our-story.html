Our story

From Shelbyville griddles to a second kitchen

The Honey Bistro is the second restaurant from Endrit and Nita Zekjiri. The first one — Star 57 Diner in Tuscola — gets a steady stream of Savoy license plates in its parking lot. So they stopped making the drive one-way.

The long road here

Nita arrived in America from North Macedonia seventeen years ago — on Thanksgiving Day, a fact she'll tell you any time you ask. She learned breakfast service early, at Lakeview Family Restaurant in Shelbyville. Years later came the call to take over the old diner in Tuscola — the former Denny's Classic — and turn it into Star 57 Diner, which opened in 2025 and hasn't slowed down since.

Every Thanksgiving, Star 57 serves a free meal from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. “It's our way of giving back to the community that supports us every day,” Nita says. “Thanksgiving is the day we arrived in America… I never want to lose sight of where I started.”

Why “Honey”

“Honestly, I just fell in love with the name,” Nita says. “I wanted something warm, sweet, welcoming, and memorable… And if you know me personally, you know I love honey!” The menu backs the name up: signature honey dishes, and honey within reach of everything else.

The promise for Savoy

“The food brings people through the doors, but it's the connections and relationships that make a place truly special,” Nita says. Same cooking, same hospitality, tighter hours — 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., planned — so both kitchens get their full attention, and Star 57 stays exactly where its regulars already love it.
